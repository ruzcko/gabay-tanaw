About this view

Select a date to update the scene, Sun and Earth positions, and daily hours together. Estimates use pinned JPL DE440 coefficients and coarse LOLA terrain. Accurate forecasts replace estimates when available. The date line keeps the selected UTC hour; the default is 00:00 UTC.

Stars follow the selected date and playback. Hipparcos catalog magnitudes set relative star brightness. Star positions are fixed at their catalog epoch; proper motion, parallax and stellar aberration are not modeled. This is a visual sky, not precision astrometry.

Earth uses the main Moon page's NASA Blue Marble imagery and diffuse sunlight style, with date-dependent, low-accuracy IAU orientation when available. Without dated orientation its rotation is illustrative; without sunlight data its texture is shown neutrally. Eclipses are not modeled. The Sun uses the same gold disc and soft glow as the main page; this is a display style, not a solar forecast.

The navigator places the Moon, Earth and Sun in one 3D scene using the selected UTC snapshot. The supplied sunlight direction lights the Moon and Earth as the date changes. Sizes and distances are schematic; Earth orientation is illustrative when dated orientation is unavailable. Click N, E, S or W to reorient the map. Hover the Moon to preview coordinates.

Coarse terrain appears first, then detailed relief loads for every catalog site and custom locations south of 60.3°S. Other custom locations retain coarse relief. Display terrain is aligned to the forecast ground height. Visibility uses the forecast horizon, independently of this display detail.

Drag or arrow keys to look; scroll or +/− to zoom.
